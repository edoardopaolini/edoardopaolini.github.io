Reading the brain as a network.

Ph.D. student in Computer Science at the University of Verona. EEG, TMS-EEG and brain connectomics for personalized epilepsy treatment.

Publications Biography

Start with the signal.

EEG records electrical potentials from electrodes on the scalp. Correlate the channels and the recording becomes a graph: electrodes are nodes, edges say how much two channels move together.

Press an electrode to deliver a pulse and watch it spread.

A cell is a graph too.

Metabolism is a network: metabolites are nodes, reactions are edges, and flux flows through them. My master's thesis modeled flux dynamics in Mycobacterium tuberculosis.

Knock out a reaction: some have a bypass and the flux reroutes, some strand the carbon.

Underneath, it is a matrix.

Every graph on this page is a 19 by 19 adjacency matrix. Same data, different question. This is where computer science does the work.

Interactive figure. A top view of a head with the 19 electrodes of the 10-20 EEG montage and simulated EEG traces. Activating an electrode delivers a simulated pulse that spreads through the connectivity graph. As you scroll, the same nodes are rearranged as a metabolic network and then as an adjacency matrix.

Simulated figures. No patient data.

What is real on this page.

The figures are toy models written in plain JavaScript for this page. They show the kind of questions my research asks. They do not report results, and none of the signals come from patients.

The degrees, the theses and the papers are real, and every paper links to its source.

The site runs on Jekyll and vanilla JavaScript, with no framework or theme. The code is on GitHub.
